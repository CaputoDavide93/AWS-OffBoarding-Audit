Optional guide

Reading this report without a technical background

A short guide to severity, suspicious signals, and the limits of automated judgement.

What this report is. It lists what this person's AWS account was used to do, automatically labelled by how much damage that kind of action could cause if misused — not by whether anything was actually misused. Software cannot read intent from a log. A “critical” item means “this is worth a human asking a question,” not “this person did something wrong.”

How to read the four levels. Critical — could grant lasting access or destroy something, and here it happened after they left. Ask about it the same day. High — a meaningful change to access or security settings; worth a quick check against a ticket. Medium — unusual enough to log, rarely worth a conversation on its own. Low — routine day-to-day activity.

What actually makes something suspicious. Severity alone doesn't. Look for the combination: no matching change ticket or handover note, activity that doesn't match this person's normal job, denied or repeatedly-retried attempts (a sign of someone probing rather than working), and — for anyone who has left — anything at all happening after their last working day. One of these is a question. Several together are a reason to escalate.
